## 1. Executive summary

Digital Fortuna Computing is a digital marketing and software company building AI-powered tools for social media advertising. Its flagship product, AdGenie, generates ad images, video and copy for TikTok, Meta and other platforms. The company pairs the product with a done-for-you marketing service and, from 2027, a managed AI back-office offering built on the JARVIS agent system.

The strategy is to win a narrow, underserved market first: African small and medium businesses that sell through social media and WhatsApp. These customers need fast, affordable, locally relevant ads, and the global AI ad tools were not built for them. Revenue comes from three connected lines: AdGenie subscriptions and credits, retainer-based marketing services, and managed JARVIS deployments.

The near-term goal is a focused, honest public launch before the end of 2026, real paying customers in Q1 2027, and a fundable or sellable business by mid-2027.

**Why now.** Ghana had about 24.3 million internet users in January 2025, 1.87 million business establishments in 2024, and roughly GH¢4.5 trillion of mobile money transactions in 2025 (provisional). Most of those businesses are small and already sell through social media and mobile money, yet the AI ad tools available to them are priced and designed for Western buyers. ## 5. Positioning and moat

Do not compete head-on with well-funded global tools. Differentiate on:

- **Local relevance.** Templates, offers and styles built for African SMEs.
- **Language.** Copy and voiceover in Twi, Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba, Swahili and French.
- **WhatsApp-first flows.** Click-to-chat ads and conversation templates that close sales.
- **Local payments.** Mobile money and cedi billing alongside USD cards.
- **Trust and security.** Bank-grade engineering practices, valuable when selling to fintechs and regulated firms.
- **Service plus software.** Customers who want results, not tools, can buy the managed option.

**Why credits, not unlimited.** Every image and video costs real money to generate. A flat price with no cap lets one heavy user cost more than they pay, and a free tier with no card invites abuse. Credits tie revenue to cost, and top-up packs capture heavy users instead of losing money on them.

Gross margin follows from the worksheet:

```latex
\text{gross margin} = \frac{\text{price} - \text{AI cost} - \text{payment fees}}{\text{price}}
```

The sensitivity is steep. If AI cost is the only cost, the margin you keep is simple to read off.

| AI cost as a share of price | Margin before payment fees |
| --- | --- |
| 20% | 80% |
| 40% | 60% |
| 60% | 40% |
| 80% | 20% |

Decide the lowest margin you will accept before you set any price, then set credits so the heaviest plausible user stays above it.

**Abuse and cost controls.** Verify email, require a payment method for video, cap daily generations per account, queue video jobs, and alert when spend per user passes a limit.

**Data protection is the urgent item.** The Data Protection Commission requires every data controller to register before processing personal data, and processing without registration is an offence under sections 53 and 56 of the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) ([DPC registration page](https://dataprotection.org.gh/registration/)). Registration is renewed every two years with a compliance report. The Commission said it would begin spot checks and enforcement, including prosecution where necessary, from 5 October 2026 ([Ghana News Agency](https://gna.org.gh/2026/10/dpc-begins-checks-on-data-protection-compliance/)).

AdGenie handles account details and uploaded images, and JARVIS may touch client email and customer data, so both fall within the Act. A lawyer should confirm whether you act as a controller, a processor or both for each product.

| AdGenie | Paying customers × monthly price + top-ups | AI generation, hosting, payment fees, support | Cost per generation, conversion, churn |

| JARVIS setups and care plans | Setups sold × fee + care plans × monthly fee | Your time, hosting, API costs | Hours per setup, hosting cost per client |

**Three formulas investors will check.**

```latex
\text{LTV} = \frac{\text{monthly revenue per customer} \times \text{gross margin}}{\text{monthly churn}}
```

```latex
\text{CAC payback (months)} = \frac{\text{CAC}}{\text{monthly revenue per customer} \times \text{gross margin}}
```

```latex
\text{runway (months)} = \frac{\text{cash in the bank}}{\text{monthly net burn}}
```

**What revenue could look like.** This is arithmetic on assumed customer counts and today's list prices, not a forecast. It shows the scale of AdGenie subscriptions alone; services and JARVIS revenue come on top.

| Paying customers | Share of Ghana's 1.87 million establishments | Annual revenue at $19 a month | Annual revenue at $39 a month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 0.005% | $22,800 | $46,800 |
| 500 | 0.027% | $114,000 | $234,000 |
| 1,000 | 0.053% | $228,000 | $468,000 |

Even 1,000 paying customers is a tiny share of the market, which is why the realistic limit is how many customers you can win and keep, not how many exist. A model that assumes churn, discounts and free-to-paid conversion will land lower than this table.

**Costs to include.** Founder pay (or a stated zero), contractors, AI and hosting, payment fees, marketing spend, legal and accounting, Data Protection Commission and trademark fees, insurance, and a cash reserve. Show the month the business first covers its own costs.

## 13. Funding strategy: how to get investors

I am not a lawyer or financial advisor, so treat this as a framework and check specifics with qualified advisers.

**What investors fund.** At your stage, investors back evidence: paying customers, growth, retention and a founder who can execute. A live app and a good deck are not enough on their own. The strongest move is to arrive with revenue, even small, and a clear story of what it will grow into.

**Sequence.**

1. **Customers first.** Reach your first paying customers and pilot results. This also funds you and gives you leverage.
2. **Non-dilutive and low-dilution options.** Accelerators and programmes such as Y Combinator, Techstars, MEST, and Google for Startups Africa, plus grants and competitions. Check each programme's current eligibility and deadlines.
3. **Angels.** Founders and operators in your fintech and capital-markets network, diaspora investors, and pan-African angel networks. Warm introductions convert far better than cold messages.
4. **Pre-seed funds.** Only after you can show traction. Early funds typically want a clear niche, repeatable acquisition and a path to scale beyond one country.

**Ask size.** Raise enough for 12 to 18 months covering product, a small team, marketing and legal: \[amount to be set from your financial model\]. Do not state a valuation until advisers and comparable deals support it.

**Instruments.** Options include convertible notes, SAFEs and priced equity. Which works in Ghana depends on legal structure and investor preference, so take advice before choosing.

**Regulation.** Offering shares to the public is regulated. Keep early rounds to private, known investors and get legal advice on Ghanaian securities rules before approaching anyone.

**The pitch.** Three sentences: who the customer is, what painful problem you solve, and the evidence it is working. Then a 10 to 12 slide deck: problem, solution, demo, market, traction, business model, go-to-market, competition, team, financials, ask.

**Outreach plan.**

- Build a list of 50 target investors and programmes, ranked by fit.
- Map your connections to each and request warm introductions.
- Send a short email with the three-sentence pitch, a link to the live product and two traction numbers.
- Track every conversation in your CRM and follow up on a schedule.

**Common mistakes to avoid.** Overstating features, showing third-party brand content, raising before having any customers, giving away equity cheaply to the first interested person, and hiding the licensing and IP questions.

**The funding landscape in numbers.** Capital is available but concentrated, and Ghana's rounds are small.

## 14. Exit options

Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers and FE International list online businesses for sale. Buyers generally want proven recurring revenue, documented operations, clean code and a business that can run without the founder. A product with no revenue sells for little, so build revenue first. Strategic acquisition by a marketing platform or agency network is another route once you have a distinct niche and customer base.

**What the marketplaces actually serve.** One 2026 review of live Acquire.com listings and closed deals describes a market of operator buyers and small search funds shopping businesses under $5 million, with most deals closing under $2 million ([Beancount.io guide](https://beancount.io/uk/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)). That is a secondary source, and I could not confirm specific valuation multiples for very small software businesses from a primary dataset, so this plan quotes none. Get live comparables from the platforms themselves before setting any price expectation.

**Exit routes compared.**

| Route | Likely buyer | What they pay for | What you need ready |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Online marketplace sale (Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers, FE International) | Operators, small funds, individual buyers | Proven recurring revenue, profit, clean handover | Several months of steady revenue, clean accounts, documented operations, transferable code |
| Strategic sale | Marketing platforms, agencies, payment or telecom firms in Africa | Customers, local-language capability, distribution | A distinct niche, retained customers, a team or documentation that survives your exit |
| Acqui-hire | Larger tech firms | Founder and team | A track record and a product that works |
| Keep and grow | None | Cash flow and control | Margin and retention strong enough to fund growth |

**Sale-readiness checklist.** Monthly revenue and churn for at least several months; AI and hosting costs tracked per customer; code in a repository the company owns, with licence confirmations; security summary; documented procedures for support, billing and deployment; customer contracts and consents; a clear story of why the business can run without you.

A sale is a realistic outcome only after revenue is proven. Until then the work is the same as for investors: real customers, measured results and clean paperwork.

## 15. Risks and mitigations

- **Brand and copyright misuse by users:** content filters, clear policies, fast takedown.
- **AI cost and provider changes:** credits, usage caps, multiple model providers.
- **Competition from large global tools:** stay focused on local niche and service.
- **Founder bandwidth:** limit scope, automate with JARVIS, consider a part-time operator or contractors.
- **Licensing and IP gaps:** resolve in writing now.
- **Data protection:** consent, minimal access, approval steps for sensitive actions.
- **Over-promising:** only claim what is built and measured.

**Risk register.** Likelihood and impact are my judgement from the findings above, to be re-rated once pilots produce evidence.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brand and copyright misuse by users or in your own demos | High | High | Original demo brands, acceptable-use policy, content filters, fast takedowns |
| AI cost overrun on uncapped plans | High | High | Credits, daily caps, spend alerts, fallback provider |
| Founder bandwidth across several ventures | High | High | Narrow scope, JARVIS for admin, contractors, hold the gates |
| Competition from global tools | High | Medium | Local niche, service layer, mobile money billing |
| Data protection breach or non-registration | Medium | High | Register now; enforcement began 5 October 2026 (section 10) |
| Licence or IP gap in course code or JARVIS | Medium | High | Written confirmations, IP assignment before any raise |
| Over-claiming features or traction | Medium | High | Verify each claim; second person reviews every investor document |
| Dependence on one model provider | Medium | Medium | Abstraction layer and a second provider |
| Cedi volatility against USD costs | Medium | Medium | Price export plans in USD; review cedi prices quarterly |
| Security incident | Low to medium | High | Security baseline, independent penetration test before selling to banks |
